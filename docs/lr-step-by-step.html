Why a GOTO right after the pop?

    
The GOTO is the second half of the reduce. A reduce replaces the right side of a rule with its left side: for E → n, the n goes away and an E takes its place. That happens in two moves:

    
    
      
  1. Pop: the states that stood for n leave the stack. We are back in the state we were in before the rule started, here state 0.
    2. 
      
  2. GOTO: the E we just built must now be placed, as if we had just read it from state 0. In the automaton, state 0 has an arrow E to state 1: GOTO[0, E] = 1 is that arrow.
    3. 
    

    
So a GOTO is a shift, but for a non-terminal. There are two tables because terminals (n, +, $) come from the text, and ACTION holds their arrows. Non-terminals (E) are never read from the text: only a reduce makes them, so GOTO, which holds their arrows, is used only there, right after the pop.

    
A reduce reads no token: the + is still the current token. Right after the GOTO, ACTION is looked up again with that same + (step 9), and this time it says shift.
